4D and 3D Imaging, Illustrated

Answer first: 3D imaging rebuilds a still scene you can walk around in. 4D imaging is that same scene plus time — the scene itself moves, so you can walk around inside a moving moment.

Study illustration only — read this first. The diagrams below are drawings that explain the ideas. This page does not load a real Gaussian splat file, does not run a WebGPU splat viewer, and is not a photorealistic render. It is a curriculum study page on GitHub Pages, not a blog post, and it changes nothing about the Blogger vetoes: no live 3D hero, no autoplay video-loop hero, and no parallax hero on a referral post.

1. What a "splat" is

3D Gaussian Splatting (SIGGRAPH 2023, Kerbl and colleagues) does not build a scene from polygons. It rebuilds it from hundreds of thousands to millions of small, soft, coloured ellipsoids — Gaussians — each with a position, a shape, a colour, and a transparency. Projected and blended in depth order, they look near-photographic and render in real time.

2. From 3D to 4D: add time

4D Gaussian Splatting adds a time axis. The splats themselves move and deform from frame to frame, instead of the camera merely moving around a frozen scene. That is what makes free-viewpoint video possible: a recorded performance you can orbit while it plays.

Newer work tries to fix the weak spots of each family: FreeTimeGS (CVPR 2025 lineage) lets Gaussians appear at arbitrary times and places with their own motion function, instead of warping one canonical scene; Instant4D (2025) rebuilds casual, uncalibrated video in minutes with grid pruning; EvoGS (December 2025) treats the scene as a continuous-time dynamical system, so motion can in principle be predicted beyond the observed frames. These are paper and vendor claims about their own benchmarks, not guarantees for any scene.

3. How 4D is captured: a rig, not a phone

The honest cost of 4D is capture, not rendering. Production capture uses synchronized multi-camera arrays — one reported NAB 2026 demo used about 60 cameras — with cameras assigned to regions of interest (a face gets more resolution than a background). A single moving phone plus a moving subject is still deeply under-constrained: the result may look plausible and be wrong.

5. A plain CSS cube, for scale

Below is the cheapest possible "3D" on a web page: a cube drawn with CSS transforms only, no JavaScript, no downloaded 3D library, no splat file. It spins with transform only and stands still for reduced-motion readers. Compare that cost with a real splat viewer — millions of primitives, GPU sorting, and a runtime — and the playbook's rule becomes obvious: a still WebP hero gives a money post the same visual information at a fraction of the cost.

6. What to keep, what to drop

  • Keep: splats as a mental model — scenes as soft points, time as a fourth axis, capture as the real cost.
  • Keep: the standards direction — glTF and OpenUSD support means study files will stay readable.
  • Drop: any plan to put a live splat viewer, a moving 4D scene, or a parallax depth effect above the answer on a referral post. The vetoes stand.
  • Drop: single-phone 4D capture as a production method in 2026 — treat it as experimental.
