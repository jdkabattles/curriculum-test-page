Advanced 3D/4D Imaging: Standards, Strategies, Methods

Answer first: the advanced work is not a prettier splat. It is making splats small, streamable, standard, and light-aware — compress them, stream only the detail the camera needs, store them in a format other tools can read, and let light and shadows behave instead of baking one frozen look.

The bars and diagrams below are drawings that explain the ideas, with sizes and percentages as reported by the formats' maintainers and by standards bodies — not measurements made on this page. 1. Strategy: shrink the file before you stream it

Raw capture is not web-ready. The original PLY output stores roughly 62 float32 values per Gaussian — about 232–248 bytes each — so a million splats is roughly a quarter of a gigabyte uncompressed. The advanced pipeline, in order, is: prune, quantize, compress, tier.

Compression ladder (relative size, maintainer-reported, not to scale in the browser):

Raw PLY — baseline, ~232 bytes per splat

Prune near-transparent splats (often 30–50% of a trained scene) + quantize spherical harmonics — 5–10x smaller, reported

SPZ (Niantic, MIT, quantization + gzip) — up to ~90% smaller than PLY, reported; SPZ v4 to ~10x, and to ~18x at 3-bit spherical harmonics, per its format guide

SOG / SOGS — 15–20x smaller than PLY, per their maintainers, and fast to decode in browsers

Rule that follows: archive the PLY, deliver the compressed file. Never ship raw PLY to a web page.

2. Strategy: stream detail by distance (LOD)

Level of detail means the camera only downloads what its pixels can show: dense splats near the camera, coarse splats far away. Hierarchical 3DGS splits a large scene into chunks at different detail levels (one production guide reports 30–40% lower peak VRAM for large outdoor scenes by loading only the relevant chunks). Cesium's 3D Tiles route does the same job with a spatial index, so a city-scale splat set can stream from sub-centimetre detail down to a whole district without loading everything at once.

3. Method: three rendering pipelines, and what each buys

PipelineHow it worksWhat it buys / costs
3DGS rasterization (EWA splatting)Project Gaussians to 2D, blend in depth-sorted order, tile by tile.Fastest, real-time. Pinhole cameras only; no secondary rays.
3DGRT (ray tracing)Trace rays against Gaussians (proxy meshes + BVH), evaluate at maximum response along the ray.Complex cameras, reflections, refractions. Higher memory and cost.
3DGUT (Unscented Transform, NVIDIA, CVPR 2025)Replace the EWA linearisation with sigma points projected exactly under any nonlinear projection.Fisheye and rolling-shutter cameras without a per-camera Jacobian, at rasterization speed; plus a hybrid form that traces secondary rays for reflections and refractions. One comparison reports beating a dedicated fisheye method with fewer than half the particles.

The method to remember: 3DGUT does not make splats "better looking" in general. It removes a specific limitation — the pinhole assumption — and aligns rasterization with ray tracing so secondary effects become possible in the same representation.

4. Method: hybrid scenes and relighting

A splat alone cannot be touched, collided with, or relit. The advanced practice is hybrid:

  • Splats for look, mesh for touch: photoreal splats as the background, invisible simple mesh shapes for floors, walls, and collision. A splat gives no clean surface to stand on.
  • Proxy meshes for light: extract or supply a mesh, predict density, normals, and material parameters, then relight the radiance field with an HDR environment map and composite mesh objects into it — or radiance fields into a mesh scene — with soft shadows and glossy interreflections at real-time rates (ISMAR 2025 work).
  • Spherical harmonics are the view-dependent trick: they store how colour changes with viewing angle, which is why a splat scene can show specular highlights a flat mesh cannot easily fake. Quantizing them is the main compression lever — and the main quality risk.

5. Standards: from ad-hoc files to interchange formats

StandardStatus as reportedWhat it fixes
KHR_gaussian_splatting (Khronos, glTF 2.0)Release candidate, February 2026; ratification expected mid-2026 in several guides. Baseline is deliberately uncompressed and GPU-ready.One cross-platform layout for position, orientation, scale, colour (spherical harmonics), and opacity, stored as point primitives — with graceful fallback to a sparse point cloud in viewers that cannot draw splats.
KHR_gaussian_splatting_compression_spz (companion, proposed)Proposed, not yet the baseline.SPZ blobs inside glTF primitives, decompressed into attributes or passed straight to a renderer; spherical harmonics from none to three degrees by content need.
OpenUSD Particle Fields (26.03, March 2026)Native schema reported (ParticleField3DGaussianSplat / UsdVolParticleField).Splats coexist with meshes, point clouds, and volumes in one scene graph, for VFX, simulation, and robotics pipelines.
MPEG Gaussian Splat Coding (GSC)Exploration, use cases approved through the 155th MPEG meeting (July 2026), including a single-file static asset with cover image and a dynamic track delivered by HTTP adaptive streaming with a pre-rendered fallback.Interoperable compression and streaming, including a legacy fallback — the same fallback principle as the glTF point-cloud fallback.
3D Tiles 2.0 (OGC community standard, proposed)Splats proposed for inclusion.Spatial indexing + LOD streaming for geospatial-scale splat sets.

Notice the pattern across all five: every standard keeps a fallback for a viewer that cannot draw splats — a point cloud, a cover image, a pre-rendered video. That is the same discipline as the playbook's rendered-still fallback, at industry scale.

6. Advanced practice checklist

  1. Archive PLY. Deliver SPZ or SOG/SOGS. Never ship raw PLY to the web.
  2. Prune near-transparent Gaussians and quantize spherical harmonics before compressing; check the specular highlights after, not just the file size.
  3. Tier by distance and stream progressively; send coarse first, refine after.
  4. Use a mesh where touch, collision, or shadows are needed; use splats where photoreal look is needed; do not ask either to do the other's job.
  5. Choose the pipeline by camera and light, not by fashion: raster for pinhole, 3DGUT for fisheye/rolling shutter, hybrid ray tracing only where reflections or refractions are the point.
  6. Target the standard format (glTF KHR, then USD) for interchange, and re-check ratification status before depending on a wire format — several of these were release candidates, not ratified, at the time of writing.

7. What this means for a blogger

Almost nothing changes on a money post — and that is the point. A compressed, streamed, standard splat is still a runtime, a download, and a GPU job placed where the answer should be. The advanced practices make splats viable for VFX, geospatial, robotics, and product viewers. For a text-first referral post, the correct application of all of this is still a rendered still as a sized WebP hero, with the splat work living in the study sandbox. The checklist above is how to judge a splat demo, not a reason to ship one.
